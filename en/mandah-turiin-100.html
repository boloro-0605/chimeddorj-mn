From Unloading Train Cars to Oxford: On Education, Corruption, and the Limits of the State

Chimeddorj Munkhjargal · "The Rising State's 100" program · Full transcript · Updated 2026

Key Ideas

Work Prepares the Person

After finishing a countryside sum school, he unloaded cement from rail cars and kneaded dough to get by in Ulaanbaatar — a living example of his grandfather's teaching that "you are not just breaking in a leather strap; the strap is preparing you." Education is a lifelong task.

A New Wave of Technology Is Reshaping the Old Way of Life

After the three industrial revolutions, a new wave of technology is giving rise to businesses with almost no marginal cost, breaking down the mechanism that once distributed income to households through wages.

Mongolia's Way Out Is Education

As automation erases the advantage of cheap labor, Mongolia's path to development is to invest mining revenue in education and equip every Mongolian with skills that machines cannot replace.

The Mongolian Language Is Culture's Immune System

Without the Mongolian language, nothing of Mongolian culture would remain. As Japan does with its own language, bringing humanity's knowledge into one's mother tongue is a crucial node of development.

Corruption Means Decay

The true meaning of the word "corruption" is decay: the Trauma Center hospital, condemned in 2007 and left unbuilt for 13 years since, is a living example of a society eaten away by kleptocracy.

The Limits of the State and Shared Values

The more power the state accumulates, the more individual rights shrink — so the state must be limited and must protect citizens' effort and initiative. Freedom, sovereignty, and justice must never be split apart, and no party should be allowed to claim them as its own.
