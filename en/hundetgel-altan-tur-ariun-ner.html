The Article

In 2020 we, the young people of the Liberty Movement, used to hold morning gatherings with invited guests. One of our honoured guests was the late Akim guai — a great man of letters and a translator — who shared the same air with us.

One thing he said has stayed vivid and deep in my heart.

"Put yourself in order, then your household; put your household in order, then the state" — that is a teaching of Confucius. The mistaken notion that it is a bequest of Chinggis Khaan has become extremely widespread. In the Holy Lord's own teaching there is a bequest of precisely the opposite sense. Chinggis Khaan taught: "A lady has three husbands. Her first husband is the golden state. Her next is an unsullied name. The one after that is the husband she took. Follow the golden state with fortitude and an unsullied name will come of itself. If you can secure an unsullied name, the husband you took will not depart elsewhere." That, he told us, is what was bequeathed. He added that the Mongol mind moves from the magnificent to the plain, arriving at the particular by way of the general.

Turning this unforgettable teaching over as a comparison: those who mistake carrying a diploma or a certificate, oiling their hair and putting on a red tie for having put themselves in order — and who confuse maintaining their household and their companions on money from corruption and theft with having put their household in order — it is exceedingly hard to believe that such people will hold strictly to the rule of law and bring a democratic state into being.
